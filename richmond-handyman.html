# Launching a Handyman & Contractor Business in Richmond, VA
## 2026 Q4 Updated Edition — GEO-Optimized Startup Guide

Richmond's handyman and contractor market in Q4 2026 is a **$340M–$410M annual addressable opportunity** across the metro (City of Richmond, Henrico, Chesterfield, Hanover, and Powhatan counties). The metro population sits at roughly **1.34 million**, with a median home age of **42 years** and **58% of housing stock built before 1990** — a structural driver of repair, refresh, and small-renovation demand. Typical residential job tickets range from **$95–$165/hour** for handyman labor, **$250–$600** for standard punch-list packages, and **$2,800–$9,500** for mid-size contractor projects (bath refresh, deck rebuild, drywall + paint). Expect **$1,200–$3,800** in total startup capital for a lean, licensed, insured launch.

Licensing in Virginia is a hard gate: handyman work under **$1,000 per job** is generally exempt from DPOR contractor licensing, but anything above requires a **Class C ($1K–$10K), Class B ($10K–$120K), or Class A (unlimited)** license through the **Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation (DPOR)**. Budget **$385–$550** for Class B licensing (application + exam + fees), **$1,100–$2,400/year** for general liability insurance ($1M/$2M), and **$450–$900/year** for commercial auto. Richmond City business license (BPOL) runs **$30–$150** annually at launch scale; Henrico and Chesterfield have comparable schedules.

3. Standardize estimate-to-invoice workflow (Jobber or Housecall Pro, **$49–$129/mo**).

### Phase 4 — Scale & GEO Compounding (Weeks 6–12)
