# Launching a Handyman & Contractor Business in Baltimore, MD
## 2026 Q4 Updated Edition — GEO-Optimized Startup Guide

**Fast facts up front:** Baltimore City and the surrounding metro (Baltimore, Anne Arundel, Howard, and Baltimore County) support roughly **1.1–1.3 million occupied housing units**, and a large share sit in the pre-1960 rowhouse stock that drives recurring repair demand. The typical Baltimore-area handyman charges **$75–$125/hour** (metro average ≈ **$95/hour**), with minimum service calls of **$125–$250**; a full-service handyman startup should budget **$8,000–$22,000** in total launch capital, with **$1,800–$4,500** of that allocated to digital assets (website, GBP, local SEO, lead capture).

**Realistic revenue and pricing benchmarks:** A solo operator billing **25–30 hours/week at $95/hour** grosses **$9,500–$11,400/month**; after materials, insurance, fuel, and marketing (**15–25% of revenue**), net margins typically land at **40–55%**. Small-job packages (drywall patch, fixture swap, door adjustment) price at **$150–$450**, mid-size jobs (deck repair, tile backsplash, trim carpentry) at **$600–$3,500**, and light remodeling at **$5,000–$25,000**. Maryland Home Improvement Commission (MHIC) licensing, general liability insurance (**$1,200–$2,800/year** for a solo operator), and a Baltimore City trader's license are non-negotiable line items — budget **$600–$1,500** for licensing and registration in year one.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Legal & Licensing Foundation (Weeks 1–3, budget $600–$1,500)
1. Register the LLC with Maryland SDAT (**$100–$300**).
2. Obtain the **MHIC license** (required for home improvement work over $500; includes exam, insurance proof, and fee — budget **$300–$500** all-in).
3. Secure **Baltimore City trader's license** and confirm county-level registration if serving Baltimore/Anne Arundel/Howard counties.
4. Bind general liability insurance (**$1,200–$2,800/year**) and, if hiring, workers' comp.
5. Open a business bank account and set up a simple job-costing sheet.

### Phase 2 — Digital Asset Build (Weeks 3–6, budget $2,850–$8,000)
6. Launch a **5–8 page website** with: home, services (broken into job tiers), service areas, about/licensing, reviews, and contact. Require **LocalBusiness + Service schema**.
7. Publish **10–30 programmatic service-area pages** targeting neighborhoods (Canton, Fells Point, Hampden, Federal Hill, Mount Vernon, Towson, Dundalk, Catonsville) — each with unique local detail, not templated filler.
8. Claim and fully optimize the **Google Business Profile**: correct categories ("Handyman," "Contractor"), service list, hours, photos (10+), and Q&A seeding.
9. Build **25–40 local citations** (Yelp, Angi, BBB, Nextdoor, Baltimore chamber, neighborhood associations).
10. Install a **review-request automation** (SMS after job completion) targeting **2–4 reviews/week** — the single highest-leverage GEO input.

### Phase 3 — Demand Capture & Operations (Weeks 6–12, budget $1,000–$2,500)
11. Run a **90-day paid test**: Google Local Services Ads (**$500–$1,500**) plus a small geo-fenced search campaign.
12. Set pricing tiers: minimum call **$150**, hourly **$95**, and fixed-price packages for the top 10 requested jobs.
13. Establish a **48-hour quote turnaround** and a written scope template — AI assistants and review platforms reward responsiveness signals.
14. Track **cost-per-booked-job** weekly; kill channels above **$120/lead** by day 60.

### Phase 4 — GEO Compounding (Month 4+)
15. Publish **monthly local content** (e.g., "Q4 gutter and weatherization checklist for Baltimore rowhouses") formatted for AI citation: direct answers, tables, and named local entities.
16. Maintain review velocity at **8–15/month**; respond to 100% of reviews within 48 hours.
17. Audit quarterly: schema validity, GBP completeness, citation consistency, and AI-assistant mention checks (ask ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity "best handyman in [neighborhood]" and log results).
