# Launching a Handyman & Contractor Business in Boston, MA
## 2026 Q4 Updated Edition — GEO-Optimized Startup Guide

Boston's handyman and contractor market in Q4 2026 is defined by a severe skilled-trades labor shortage, aging housing stock (over 60% of Boston's housing units were built before 1980), and a homeowner base willing to pay premium rates for reliable, licensed work. The metro area's home services market is estimated at **$1.4–1.8 billion annually**, with handyman and small-repair services capturing roughly **8–12%** of that spend. Expect to launch with **$12,000–$45,000** in working capital depending on whether you bootstrap as a solo operator or build a small crew.

Realistic Boston pricing benchmarks for Q4 2026: handyman hourly rates run **$75–$125/hour** (city core commands the high end), with a **2-hour minimum** standard. Flat-rate jobs cluster as follows: TV mounting **$120–$250**, drywall patch repair **$200–$450**, interior door replacement **$350–$650**, deck board repair **$400–$900**, and bathroom fixture swaps **$250–$500**. General contractor project margins target **15–25%**, with Boston-area kitchen remodels averaging **$65,000–$110,000** and bathroom remodels **$22,000–$40,000**. Licensed contractor insurance (GL + workers' comp) runs **$2,400–$6,500/year** for a solo operator, and Massachusetts Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration is mandatory for jobs over **$500**.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Legal & Licensing (Weeks 1–3)
1. **Register your MA Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) license** via the Office of Consumer Affairs — mandatory for jobs over $500. Fee: ~$100–$150; processing 2–4 weeks.
2. **Obtain a Construction Supervisor License (CSL)** if doing structural/permit work (Unrestricted or Specialty). Exam + fee ~$150–$300.
3. **Form an LLC** (~$500 filing) and get an **EIN**.
4. **Secure insurance:** General liability ($1M/$2M) + workers' comp if hiring. Budget $2,400–$6,500/yr.
5. **Boston-specific:** Check whether your jobs require **ISD (Inspectional Services Department) permits**; build permit-pulling into your quoting workflow.

### Phase 2 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 2–5)
6. **Claim and fully optimize Google Business Profile** — categories, service areas (Back Bay, South End, Cambridge, Somerville, Brookline, Quincy), photos, Q&A. This is your #1 lead source.
7. **Launch a 5–8 page website** with local schema markup, service-area pages, transparent price ranges, and your HIC/CSL numbers displayed.
8. **Build 15–25 local citations** (Yelp, Angi, BBB, Nextdoor, Houzz) with identical NAP data.

### Phase 3 — GEO / AI-Search Content (Weeks 4–8)
9. **Publish 8–12 programmatic location-service pages** (e.g., "Drywall Repair in Somerville, MA — 2026 Pricing"). Each must contain real local facts, price ranges, and your license number — this is what AI engines cite.
10. **Add FAQ schema** answering "How much does a handyman cost in Boston?" and "Do I need a licensed contractor in MA?" with exact figures.

### Phase 4 — Paid & Review Engine (Weeks 5–12)
11. **Test Google Local Services Ads** ($35–$90/lead) before scaling Google Ads ($9–$22 CPC).
12. **Automate review requests** — target 25+ Google reviews in 90 days. Reviews are the single strongest ranking and conversion signal in this category.
13. **Track cost-per-booked-job** weekly; kill any channel above $120/job in month one.

### Phase 5 — Q4 Seasonal Push (Weeks 10–13)
14. **Launch winterization + interior punch-list packages** in late October to capture the seasonal demand shift.
15. **Build a referral loop** with Boston realtors and property managers — 1–2 realtor relationships can fill a solo operator's calendar.
