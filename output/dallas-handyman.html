## Executive Snapshot: Dallas Market Numbers (Read First)

Dallas is one of the highest-velocity home-services markets in the U.S. As of Q4 2026, the DFW metro population sits at roughly **8.1 million**, with the City of Dallas alone at ~1.35 million. Median single-family home age in core Dallas (built largely 1950s–1980s) means **constant repair demand**: the average Dallas homeowner spends **$3,200–$5,800/year** on maintenance and repairs. The median handyman service call in Dallas runs **$95–$145/hour**, with a **$180–$350 minimum job ticket** typical for licensed operators.

Startup capital realistically breaks down as: **Texas LLC formation $300**, **Dallas trade licensing/permit setup $150–$600**, **general liability insurance $1,200–$2,400/year**, **vehicle wrap $2,500–$4,500**, and **digital launch assets $1,800–$9,500** depending on build vs. buy. ## 2026 Q4 Dallas Market Analysis

### Demand Signals
- **DFW is the fastest-growing large metro in the U.S.**, adding ~100,000+ residents annually — new households need punch-list and turnover work.
- **Aging housing stock in Dallas proper**: ~60% of single-family homes in 752xx ZIPs are 30+ years old → plumbing, drywall, door, and trim repair demand.
- **Insurance-driven repair volume**: hail season (March–June) and freeze events (Jan–Feb) create Q1/Q2 spikes; Q4 is a **pre-holiday punch-list and rental-turnover window** — ideal for launching.
- **Rental density**: Dallas has a high single-family rental rate (~35%+ of housing units in many submarkets), and property managers are the highest-LTV B2B channel.

### Competitive Landscape
- **Fragmented**: thousands of solo operators, few with professional digital assets. Google Business Profile (GBP) competition in suburbs (Plano, Richardson, Garland, Irving) is **materially softer** than in central Dallas.
- **Lead costs**: Angi/HomeAdvisor/Thumbtack leads in DFW run **$25–$95 per shared lead**; conversion on shared leads is typically **8–15%**. Owned-channel leads (SEO/GBP) convert at **25–40%** and cost far less at scale.

## Brick-and-Mortar Agency vs. Verified Freelance Experts

This is the decision that most impacts your first 12 months of cash flow. Here's the honest comparison for a Dallas launch.

### Local Brick-and-Mortar Agency
**Typical DFW pricing:** $3,500–$8,000/month retainer; $15k–$40k+ for a full launch package.

**Pros**
- Single point of accountability; in-person meetings possible.
- Bundled services (design, SEO, ads, content) under one contract.
- Local market familiarity — but verify they actually serve home-services clients, not just generic SMBs.

**Cons**
- **2–4x the cost** of equivalent freelance work for the same deliverables.
- High overhead baked into retainers; you fund their office, not your growth.
- Slow turnaround on page edits and content — often 5–10 business days.
- Lock-in contracts (6–12 months) common; exit clauses favor the agency.
- Junior staff often do the actual work despite senior-level pricing.

### Verified Freelance Experts
**Typical pricing:** $40–$95/hr; project-based SEO/content packages **$1,500–$6,000**.

**Pros**
- **40–65% lower cost** for identical deliverables.
- Faster iteration (24–72 hr turnarounds common).
- You can hire specialists per channel (SEO, GBP, ads, copy) rather than a generalist bundle.
- No long-term lock-in; pay for output.

**Cons**
- Requires vetting: demand **portfolio proof in home services**, verified reviews, and a paid trial project.
- You manage coordination if using multiple freelancers.
- Quality variance is real — **always run a $250–$500 paid test project** before committing.

### Bottom Line for Dallas Launch
For a **first-year handyman business**, verified freelancers win on cost-per-result **unless** you lack the time to vet and manage them. A hybrid model — freelancer for programmatic SEO + content, DIY for GBP and reviews — delivers agency-grade output at **~35–45% of agency cost** ($6,300–$15,100 vs. $15,600–$42,200).

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Legal & Licensing (Weeks 1–2)
1. **Form a Texas LLC** via the Secretary of State ($300 filing; ~$1,500 if using a registered agent service + expedite).
2. **Obtain Dallas trade licenses/registrations** as required for your scope (general handyman work under Texas thresholds generally does not require a state contractor license, but **electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and roofing do** — stay within legal scope or partner with licensed subs). Budget $150–$600 for permits/registrations.
3. **Secure general liability insurance** ($1,200–$2,400/yr) and, if hiring, workers' comp.
4. **Open a business bank account** and set up a simple bookkeeping stack (QuickBooks/Wave).

### Phase 2 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 2–5)
5. **Claim and fully optimize your Google Business Profile** — categories, service areas (Dallas + 6–10 suburbs), hours, photos, and a review-request workflow. This is your #1 GEO asset.
6. **Build a 10-page core site**: Home, Services, Service Areas, About, Reviews, Contact, plus 3–4 high-intent service pages (drywall repair, door installation, TV mounting, rental turnover).
7. **Implement schema markup**: `LocalBusiness`, `Service`, `FAQPage`, and `AggregateRating`. This is what makes generative engines cite you.
8. **Launch programmatic SEO**: generate 50–200 pages targeting `[service] + [Dallas neighborhood/suburb]` (e.g., "drywall repair Plano TX," "handyman Richardson TX"). Use templated, genuinely differentiated content — not thin duplicates.

### Phase 3 — Trust & Reviews (Weeks 3–8, ongoing)
9. **Automate review requests** via SMS after every job; target **25+ Google reviews in 90 days** (the local-pack threshold in most Dallas suburbs).
10. **Build 30–60 citations** (Yelp, BBB, Nextdoor, Angi profile, Thumbtack, local chambers).
11. **Publish 10 neighborhood guides** ("Handyman Services in Lakewood," "Rental Turnover Help in Uptown") — these are prime AI-citation fodder.

### Phase 4 — Paid & B2B Channels (Weeks 6–12)
12. **Launch Google Local Services Ads** (pay-per-lead, Google-verified) — typically $8–$35/lead in DFW for handyman categories.
13. **Direct-outreach to property managers**: target 20–30 small PM firms in Dallas; a single PM relationship can yield **$2,000–$8,000/month** in recurring turnover work.
14. **Set a Q4 promo** (pre-holiday punch-list special) to fill the calendar during your launch window.

### Phase 5 — Measure & Scale (Month 3+)
15. Track **cost-per-booked-job** by channel; kill anything above $120/job.
16. Reinvest in the channels that produce owned leads (GBP, SEO, reviews) — these compound; paid leads do not.
