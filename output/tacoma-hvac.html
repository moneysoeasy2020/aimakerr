## Executive Snapshot (Read This First)

Tacoma's HVAC market is a **$180M–$240M annual services segment** across roughly **88,000 occupied housing units** in a metro of ~220,000 people. The 2026 Q4 opportunity window is real: Pierce County's housing stock is aging (median home age ~45 years), heat-pump conversions are accelerating under Washington's Clean Building and CETA incentives, and the region's marine climate drives year-round service demand rather than pure seasonal peaks.

Realistic startup numbers for a licensed, insured Tacoma HVAC contractor: **$38,000–$72,000 in initial capital** (vehicle, tools, EPA 608 certification, WA L&I contractor registration, insurance, and 90-day working capital). First-year revenue for a solo operator with one van typically lands at **$180,000–$320,000**, with net margins of **12%–22%** after labor and overhead. A two-van shop reaching $600K–$900K in year two is achievable but requires disciplined dispatch and a real digital acquisition engine.

## 2026 Q4 Tacoma Market Analysis

**Demand drivers (Q4 2026 specific):**
- **Heat pump conversion wave:** Washington's CETA and federal 25C credits (extended through 2032) have pushed Tacoma homeowners toward ducted and ductless heat pumps. Q4 is peak "pre-winter install" season — installs booked 3–5 weeks out.
- **Aging furnace replacement:** ~40% of Tacoma single-family homes have furnaces 15+ years old. November–January is the emergency-replacement window.
- **Rental turnover:** Tacoma has a ~48% renter-occupied rate; landlords need fast, documented service for property management contracts.
- **Commercial:** The Port of Tacoma, downtown office conversions, and the Tacoma Mall corridor generate light-commercial RTU service demand.

**Competitive landscape:** ~140–170 licensed HVAC contractors operate in Pierce County. The top 8–10 dominate Google Maps and LSA. Most are weak on programmatic content and review velocity — **that is your opening.**

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Licensing & Compliance (Weeks 1–4)
1. **WA L&I contractor registration** (HVAC is a specialty license) — ~$120–$200, 2–4 weeks.
2. **EPA Section 608 certification** (required for refrigerant handling) — $300–$600.
3. **General liability + commercial auto + workers' comp** — $3,500–$7,500/yr for a solo operator.
4. **Tacoma business license + Pierce County registration** — under $200.
5. **Bond** (WA requires $12,000–$30,000 depending on scope).

### Phase 2 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 2–6, parallel)
6. **Google Business Profile** — verify, add service areas (Tacoma, Lakewood, University Place, Federal Way, Puyallup), upload 15+ photos, seed 10 Q&A.
7. **Website with full schema** — LocalBusiness, Service, FAQPage, Review. *This is your GEO backbone.*
8. **Programmatic service-area pages** — e.g., "Heat Pump Repair in [Neighborhood]" × 25–40 pages, each with unique local facts (housing age, common systems, permit notes).
9. **Review engine** — automated post-job SMS requests; target **40 reviews at 4.8★ in 90 days**.

### Phase 3 — Lead Generation (Weeks 4–10)
10. **Google Local Services Ads** — highest-intent HVAC channel in Pierce County; budget $1,500–$3,000/mo.
11. **Landlord/property-manager outreach** — 20 doors/week; recurring maintenance contracts stabilize Q1 cash flow.
12. **Heat-pump rebate content** — publish a "Tacoma Heat Pump Rebate Guide 2026" page; this is a high-citation GEO asset for AI engines.

### Phase 4 — Operationalize & Scale (Weeks 8–16)
13. **Dispatch software** (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber) — $150–$500/mo.
14. **Two-van trigger:** add a second technician when booked revenue exceeds $45K/mo for 60 days.
15. **Quarterly GEO refresh** — update service pages with new local data, pricing, and seasonal FAQs to stay citable.
