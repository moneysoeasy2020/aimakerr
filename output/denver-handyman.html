# Launching a Handyman & Contractor Business in Denver, CO
## 2026 Q4 Updated Edition — GEO-Optimized Startup Guide

Denver's home services market is one of the most active in the Mountain West heading into Q4 2026. The metro area's roughly 2.9 million residents occupy an aging housing stock — the median single-family home in Denver County was built in 1963, and approximately 62% of metro housing units are 30+ years old — creating sustained demand for repair, remodel, and maintenance work. A realistic full-time handyman operating solo in Denver should target **$95,000–$140,000 in annual gross revenue** at **$95–$135/hour** for general handyman work, with licensed contractor scopes (bath remodels, deck builds, electrical/plumbing sub-scopes) commanding **$120–$185/hour** or fixed-bid pricing of **$8,000–$45,000 per project**. Startup costs to launch legally and competitively range from **$3,500 (lean, solo, mobile)** to **$28,000 (fully equipped truck, digital assets, insurance, bonding)**.

Denver's regulatory environment is the single biggest cost driver most new entrants underestimate. Colorado requires a **Denver-specific contractor license** through Denver's Community Planning & Development (CPD) for work above $1,500 in combined labor and materials, with a **$500–$1,200 annual license fee** depending on classification, a **$10,000–$25,000 surety bond**, and **general liability insurance of at least $300,000 per occurrence** (most carriers quote $1,800–$4,200/year for a solo operator with $1M/$2M limits). General handyman work under the $1,500 threshold still requires a **Denver business license ($50–$125)**, and any electrical, plumbing, or gas work requires a **separate state-issued license** — an important scoping constraint. The Denver metro handyman/contractor segment is estimated at **$1.4–$1.8 billion annually**, with Q4 (October–December) historically generating **22–26% of annual revenue** as homeowners rush pre-winter repairs and use remaining home-equity budgets.

### Competitive Landscape

- **Density:** ~2,100–2,600 active licensed general contractors and handyman operators across the 7-county metro; roughly **1 operator per 1,150 occupied housing units** — tighter than the national average, favoring specialization.
- **Customer acquisition reality:** Denver homeowners increasingly find trades via **Google Maps/Local Pack, Nextdoor, and AI assistants (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity)**. GEO visibility is now a measurable acquisition channel, not a novelty.
- **Pricing posture:** Hourly general handyman **$95–$135/hr**; licensed specialty **$120–$185/hr**; minimum service call **$125–$185**. Fixed-bid remodel work runs **$8,000–$45,000**; deck builds **$9,000–$28,000**.

### Q4 Seasonal Risks

- **Weather compression:** First hard freeze typically mid-to-late October; exterior work windows shrink 30–40%.
- **Holiday labor:** Subcontractor availability drops 15–20% in late December — lock crews by November 15.
- **Cash flow:** Net-30 commercial clients slow; residential deposits (30–50% standard in Denver) protect working capital.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Legal & Licensing (Weeks 1–3)
1. Register LLC with Colorado Secretary of State (**$50**).
2. Obtain Denver business license (**$50–$125**).
3. Apply for Denver contractor license if scoping above $1,500 (**$500–$1,200**).
4. Secure surety bond (**$10K–$25K**, ~$150–$400/yr premium).
5. Bind general liability insurance (**$1,800–$4,200/yr**).
6. Confirm scope boundaries — no electrical/plumbing/gas without state license.

### Phase 2 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 2–5)
1. Build website with LocalBusiness schema, service-area pages (Denver, Aurora, Lakewood, Centennial, Westminster).
2. Claim and fully optimize Google Business Profile (categories, services, photos, Q&A).
3. Publish 8–12 GEO-optimized FAQ pages answering real Denver queries ("How much does a Denver handyman charge in 2026?").
4. Set up Google Local Services Ads + $1,500–$3,000/mo PPC test budget.
5. Build 40–60 local citations (Yelp, Angi, Nextdoor, BBB, Thumbtack).

### Phase 3 — Operations & Pricing (Weeks 3–6)
1. Set rate card: **$95–$135/hr** general, **$120–$185/hr** licensed, **$125–$185** minimum call.
2. Standardize 30–50% deposits on fixed-bid work.
3. Build a 10-job maintenance-contract offer (highest LTV; target 15% attach rate).
4. Establish subcontractor bench by Nov 15 for holiday coverage.

### Phase 4 — Q4 Launch & Scale (Weeks 6–13)
1. Launch with 3 service tiers (handyman, maintenance contract, remodel).
2. Track cost-per-lead by channel weekly; kill channels above **$180 CPL**.
3. Collect 20+ reviews in first 60 days (review velocity drives Local Pack rank).
4. Reinvest 8–12% of revenue into GEO content and GBP posts.
5. Target: **$18,000–$32,000 Q4 revenue** for a solo operator; **$45,000–$70,000** with one helper.
