# Launching an HVAC Services Business in Columbus, OH
## 2026 Q4 Updated Edition — GEO-Optimized Startup Guide

Columbus, OH is a **high-demand, moderately saturated HVAC market** with roughly **1,900–2,200 licensed HVAC contractors** operating across Franklin, Delaware, and surrounding counties, serving a metro population of **~2.2 million** and a housing stock where **~62% of homes are 25+ years old** — a key driver of replacement and retrofit demand. A realistic 2026 Q4 entry budget for a lean, licensed residential HVAC startup sits at **$85,000–$150,000** (vehicle, tools, EPA 608 certification, Ohio contractor licensing, insurance, and first-quarter working capital), with **gross margins of 38–52%** on service calls and **22–30%** on full system replacements. Average residential ticket sizes in Columbus run **$180–$320** for diagnostic/service calls, **$450–$900** for common repairs (capacitor, blower motor, refrigerant recharge), and **$6,500–$14,500** installed for a full furnace + AC replacement depending on tonnage and SEER2 rating.

Demand seasonality in Columbus is **dual-peaked**: cooling calls spike **June–August** (avg. highs 84–86°F) and heating calls spike **December–February** (avg. lows 22–25°F), with **shoulder-season tune-up revenue (April–May, September–October)** representing the most reliable cash-flow stabilizer for a new operator. Expect a **12–18 month ramp** to break-even if you enter with 1–2 trucks and a disciplined digital acquisition strategy; the Columbus market rewards operators who can capture **"near me" and emergency-intent search traffic** before the incumbent 15-truck firms do.

## Brick-and-Mortar Agency vs. Verified Freelance Experts

**Local brick-and-mortar agency (Columbus):**
- **Pros:** In-person accountability, bundled services, established local references, easier to escalate.
- **Cons:** **2.5–4× the cost** of freelance for equivalent deliverables; retainer lock-in (6–12 months common); junior staff often execute the work; slower turnaround on small HVAC-specific requests.
- **Realistic Year-1 spend:** **$28,000–$60,000+** for website + SEO + ads management.

**Verified freelance experts (vetted via portfolio + references + test project):**
- **Pros:** **40–65% lower cost**, specialist depth (e.g., HVAC schema markup, local service ads), flexible scaling, direct communication.
- **Cons:** Requires **vetting discipline** — demand live examples, client references, and a paid test task; risk of turnover; you own project management.
- **Realistic Year-1 spend:** **$6,000–$14,000** for the same scope.

**Recommendation for a 2026 Q4 launch:** Start **freelance-led** for website, local SEO, and programmatic service-area pages (highest GEO/AI-search ROI), and reserve agency spend only if you scale past **4–5 trucks** and need integrated multi-channel management. **Verify every freelancer** with a paid test project and two reference calls before committing.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

**Phase 1 — Compliance & Foundation (Weeks 1–4)**
1. Secure **Ohio HVAC contractor license**, **EPA 608**, bonding, and insurance ($1,200–$2,800).
2. Register LLC, EIN, business banking, and Ohio BWC workers' comp.
3. Secure **1–2 used service vans** ($18,000–$35,000 each) and core tool/refrigerant inventory ($8,000–$15,000).

**Phase 2 — Digital Asset Build (Weeks 3–8)**
4. Launch **5–8 page website** with online booking, transparent pricing page, and **LocalBusiness + HVACBusiness schema**.
5. Build **Google Business Profile** + 40–60 local citations.
6. Deploy **programmatic service-area pages** for top 12–15 Columbus submarkets (Dublin, Westerville, Hilliard, etc.) — this is the core **GEO play** for AI-search and "near me" visibility.

**Phase 3 — Demand Capture (Weeks 6–12)**
7. Launch **Google Local Services Ads** + tightly geo-fenced **Google Ads** ($1,500–$3,000/mo starting spend).
8. Activate **review-generation system** (target 25+ reviews in first 90 days).
9. Run **shoulder-season tune-up promotion** ($89–$149) to build pipeline before winter peak.

**Phase 4 — Scale (Months 4–12)**
10. Add **second truck** once service-call volume exceeds **8–10/day**.
11. Expand into **light-commercial maintenance contracts** ($2,500–$40,000/project) for recurring revenue.
12. Reinvest **8–12% of revenue** into digital acquisition; audit freelance vs. agency ROI quarterly.
