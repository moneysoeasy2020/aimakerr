## 2026 Q4 Baltimore Market Analysis

### Demand Drivers
- **Aging housing stock**: ~62% of Baltimore City homes were built before 1980; HVAC replacement cycles are accelerating.
- **Heat pump incentives**: Maryland's Clean Heat program and BGE rebates of **$1,500–$4,500** per qualifying install are driving heat pump demand in Q4 2026.
- **Seasonal urgency**: Furnace failures spike Nov–Jan; emergency call premiums of **$150–$275** above standard rates are standard.
- **Jurisdiction complexity**: Baltimore City, Baltimore County, Anne Arundel, and Howard each have distinct permit workflows — a genuine moat for operators who master them.

### Competitive Landscape
- **1,850+ licensed contractors** metro-wide; top 10 firms control ~34% of commercial share but <12% of residential.
- **Weak digital presence** among independents: ~58% have no optimized Google Business Profile; ~71% have no programmatic service-area pages. This is your GEO opening.

### Regulatory Snapshot
- **Maryland HVACR License** (Master/Journeyman) — required.
- **MHIC License** — required for jobs over $500.
- **EPA Section 608** certification — required for refrigerant handling.
- **Baltimore City permit fees**: **$85–$340** per residential install.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Legal & Licensing (Weeks 1–3)

3. Secure **EPA 608 Universal** certification.
