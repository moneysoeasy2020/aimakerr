The city's housing stock is unusually split: about **58% of units are renter-occupied**, and a large share of the owner-occupied base sits in pre-1950 rowhouses and brownstones in the Heights, Journal Square, and Bergen-Lafayette — buildings that need boiler, steam, and ductless mini-split expertise more than standard forced-air work. ## 2026 Q4 Jersey City Market Analysis

### Demand drivers specific to Jersey City
- **Aging systems:** A majority of the owner-occupied rowhouse stock was built before 1960; boilers and steam systems in the Heights and Bergen-Lafayette are entering replacement cycles en masse through 2028.
- **Renter-heavy market:** 58% renter occupancy means landlord accounts (multi-family, 2–4 unit buildings) are a larger share of serviceable revenue than in typical suburbs. Landlord pricing is volume-based: expect **$85–$120/service call** with 24–48 hour response commitments.
- **New construction corridor:** Journal Square, Paulus Hook, and the waterfront have added thousands of high-rise units since 2020; these buildings are served by property-management contracts and BMS vendors, not small operators — but they generate steady **mini-split and VRF service demand** from condo owners.
- **Climate:** Hudson County sits in ASHRAE climate zone 4A. Heating degree days hover near **4,900/year** and cooling degree days near **1,250/year** — roughly balanced heating/cooling revenue, unlike warmer metros.

### Competitive landscape
- **Established incumbents:** 25–40 active HVAC contractors serve Hudson County; the top 5 capture an estimated 40–50% of residential replacement revenue.
- **Differentiation levers that work in Jersey City:** boiler/steam expertise (scarce), Spanish-language service (roughly 28% of Jersey City residents speak Spanish at home), same-day emergency response, and transparent flat-rate pricing.
- **Seasonality:** Q4 (Oct–Dec) is heating-emergency season; Q1 is the best window to sell pre-season AC contracts.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Licensing & Compliance (Weeks 1–6)
1. **NJ HVACR license:** Obtain the New Jersey HVACR Master license (or employ a licensed master) via the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs — required to contract HVAC work in NJ. Budget **$500–$1,200** in fees, exam, and application.
2. **Jersey City business registration:** Register with the Jersey City Clerk, obtain a **Mercantile/Contractor license** (~$100–$300), and confirm zoning for any yard/warehouse space.
3. **Insurance:** General liability **$1M/$2M** (~$1,800–$3,200/yr) plus commercial auto for two vans (~$4,500–$7,000/yr in Hudson County).
4. **EPA Section 608 certification** for refrigerant handling (required for all techs).
5. **Hudson County Construction Board of Appeals** awareness for permit-pulling on replacements.

### Phase 2 — Capital & Fleet (Weeks 4–8)
- Two used service vans: **$18,000–$30,000** combined
- Tools and recovery equipment: **$6,000–$9,000**
- Initial parts inventory: **$4,000–$7,000**
- Working capital reserve: **$8,000–$12,000** (critical — HVAC receivables lag)

### Phase 3 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 5–10)
1. **Google Business Profile** — verify at your Jersey City address; category "HVAC contractor"; service areas: Jersey City, Hoboken, Bayonne, Union City, Weehawken.
2. **Website** with schema markup (`HVACBusiness`, `LocalBusiness`, `Service`) — non-negotiable for GEO/AI-search citation.
3. **Programmatic service-area pages:** build **40–80 pages** combining service type × neighborhood (e.g., "boiler repair in the Heights," "mini-split installation Journal Square," "AC repair Paulus Hook"). This is the highest-leverage GEO asset for AI-generated local answers.
4. **Review engine:** automated post-job SMS review requests; target **25+ Google reviews in 90 days**.

### Phase 4 — Demand Generation (Weeks 8–16)
- Launch Google Local Services Ads (**$18–$35/lead** typical in Hudson County HVAC) alongside standard search.
- Landlord outreach: 2–4 unit building owners in Bergen-Lafayette and McGinley Square; offer maintenance contracts at **$180–$280/unit/year**.
- Seasonal Q4 push: heating tune-up specials at **$179** to build the customer list before winter emergencies.

### Phase 5 — Stabilize & Scale (Months 5–12)
- Target: **15–25 inbound leads/month** by month 9; **$350K–$550K** year-one revenue for a two-truck operation.
- Track cost-per-booked-job; benchmark **$120–$220** blended.
- Reinvest in a third tech once monthly revenue clears **$60K**.
