A licensed sole-proprietor handyman operating 5 days/week in SF can realistically gross **$180,000–$320,000/year** at blended rates of **$95–$165/hour** (labor), with small-project contractor work (bathrooms, decks, ADU prep) commanding **$12,000–$85,000** per job.

### Agency vs. Freelance: The Explicit Breakdown

- **Brick-and-mortar agency:** Higher overhead (office, account managers, sales team), bundled deliverables, slower turnaround (2–4 weeks per change request), but single point of accountability. Best if you have zero time and a $5K+/mo budget.
- **Verified freelance expert:** 55–70% lower cost, faster iteration (24–72 hr turnarounds), direct communication. **Verification checklist:** CSLB-adjacent marketing experience, 3+ local service-business case studies with rankings, signed scope-of-work contract, milestone-based payments (never 100% upfront), and access to your own Google Business Profile, GSC, and domain — never let a vendor own your assets.
- **Hybrid recommendation for 2026 Q4:** Freelancer for website + GBP + programmatic SEO; agency only if you scale to 3+ crews and need full-funnel management.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Legal & Licensing (Weeks 1–4)
1. **Decide tier:** Handyman (under $500/job labor, no structural/electrical/plumbing permits) vs. licensed contractor (CSLB Class B General or C-39 Roofing etc.). Most SF startups begin as handyman and upgrade to Class B within 12–18 months.
2. **File CSLB application** ($450 sole owner; fingerprinting ~$50–$80; 4–6 week processing in 2026).
3. **Secure $25,000 contractor bond** (~$350–$650/yr) if licensed.
4. **General liability insurance** ($1,800–$4,200/yr for solo, 1M/2M).
5. **SF business registration (TTX)** + Seller's Permit if selling materials; **~$100–$500/yr** by gross receipts.
6. **Open business bank account** and separate books (required for bonding/insurance audits).

### Phase 2 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 3–6)
7. **Domain + hosting** ($15–$40/mo) and a 5–8 page site with **LocalBusiness + Service schema**, service-area pages, and click-to-call.
8. **Google Business Profile** — verify, add 10+ photos, service categories, Q&A, and review request flow. This alone drives **40–60% of inbound leads** for SF handymen.
9. **Programmatic SEO layer:** generate **40–80 pages** targeting `[service] + [SF neighborhood]` (e.g., "drywall repair Mission District," "deck repair Noe Valley") — template with unique 150–250 word intros, local landmarks, and pricing ranges.
10. **Review engine:** automated SMS/email review requests; target **25+ Google reviews in 90 days** (SF buyers filter at 4.5★+).

### Phase 3 — Lead Generation & Launch (Weeks 5–8)
11. **Google Local Services Ads** ($28–$75/lead in SF) — start at **$1,500/mo** budget; LSA is pay-per-lead and Google-guaranteed.
12. **Google PPC** on high-intent terms ($1,200–$2,500/mo, 8–12% of spend to management).
13. **Thumbtack/Angi** as bridge channels ($300–$800/mo) until organic ranks.
14. **Neighborhood partnerships:** hardware stores (Cole Hardware, Ace), property managers, real estate agents — 3–5 referral relationships in month one.

### Phase 4 — Scale & Optimize (Months 3–6)
15. **Track cost-per-lead and close rate weekly** (target CPL < $80, close rate > 35%).
16. **Add a second crew** once booked utilization exceeds **75%** for 8 consecutive weeks.
17. **Refresh programmatic pages quarterly** with 2026 Q4 pricing and permit notes (SF DBI permit timelines: 2–8 weeks for minor projects).
