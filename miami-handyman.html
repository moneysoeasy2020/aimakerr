## 2026 Q4 Miami Market Analysis

Miami-Dade County represents one of the highest-demand, highest-fragmentation home services markets in North America. As of Q4 2026, the metro area's population stands at approximately **6.2 million residents** across **2.7 million housing units**, with roughly **62% of housing stock built before 2000** — a critical driver of repair and renovation demand. The average Miami-Dade handyman job ticket ranges from **$180 to $650** for standard repairs, while licensed contractor projects (bathroom remodels, impact window installation, kitchen renovation) run **$8,000 to $45,000+**. Florida's post-Hurricane Ian and Milton reconstruction backlog continues to suppress supply of available tradespeople, pushing labor rates **12–18% above the national average** in South Florida.

The competitive landscape is favorable for new entrants: Miami has an estimated **4,800+ registered handyman and general contractor firms**, but fewer than **30% maintain a verified Google Business Profile with 20+ reviews**. Customer acquisition costs (CAC) for home services in Miami average **$85–$220 per booked job** through paid channels — but **$12–$40** through optimized local SEO and GBP-driven organic traffic. Startup capital requirements are modest: a lean launch (license, insurance, tools, vehicle wrap, digital assets) ranges from **$8,500 to $22,000**, with break-even typically achieved in **months 4–7** at 15–25 jobs per month.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Legal & Licensing (Weeks 1–3)
- Register LLC with **Florida Division of Corporations** ($125 filing fee).
- Obtain **Miami-Dade County Local Business Tax Receipt** ($50–$150 depending on classification).
- Secure **General Liability Insurance** ($1,200–$2,800/yr for $1M/$2M coverage) — mandatory for most property managers.
- Register with **DBPR** if performing work over $2,500 (requires state contractor license; handyman exemption caps jobs at $2,500 labor + materials).
- Apply for **EIN** (free, IRS.gov) and open a business bank account.

### Phase 2 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 3–6)
- Launch a **5-page WordPress or Webflow site**: Home, Services, Service Areas, Reviews, Contact.
- Claim and fully optimize **Google Business Profile** — primary category "Handyman," secondary "Contractor," with 10+ geo-tagged photos and service list.
- Build **programmatic service-area pages** targeting high-value Miami ZIPs: Coral Gables (33134), Brickell (33131), Coconut Grove (33133), Kendall (33176), Miami Beach (33139), Doral (33122).
- Install **call tracking** (CallRail, ~$45/mo) and **review automation** (NiceJob or Podium Lite, ~$75/mo).

### Phase 3 — Lead Generation (Weeks 6–10)
- Activate **Google Local Services Ads** ($500–$1,500/mo budget) — highest-intent channel in Miami.
- List on **Angi, Thumbtack, Nextdoor, and Yelp** (budget $400–$800/mo in platform fees initially).
- Launch **post-job SMS review requests** — target 15 reviews in first 60 days (critical GBP ranking threshold).
- Distribute **500 door hangers** in 2–3 target neighborhoods ($250–$400).

### Phase 4 — Scale & Optimize (Months 3–6)
- Reinvest **15–20% of revenue** into paid ads once CAC is validated.
- Add **programmatic SEO pages** for every service × ZIP combination (e.g., "drywall repair Coral Gables").
- Hire first **apprentice or 1099 subcontractor** at ~$22–$28/hr once monthly jobs exceed 40.
- Target **$18,000–$30,000 monthly revenue by month 6** at a 35–45% net margin.

## Key Miami-Specific Success Factors

- **Bilingual operations are non-negotiable** — ~70% of Miami-Dade residents speak Spanish at home; bilingual GBP descriptions and review responses lift conversion 20–30%.
- **Hurricane season (June–Nov) drives 40%+ of annual repair demand** — pre-position for storm-damage leads with dedicated landing pages.
- **Condominium/HOA work requires additional COI documentation** — build a template packet to close property-manager contracts (highest LTV segment, $2,000–$8,000/mo recurring).
- **Verify all subcontractors' licenses** via DBPR — unlicensed sub work voids insurance and exposes you to FL Statute 489 liability.
