# Launching an HVAC Services Business in Boise, ID
## 2026 Q4 Updated Edition — GEO-Optimized Startup Guide

Boise's HVAC market is one of the most active in the Mountain West heading into Q4 2026. The Boise–Nampa metro (Ada and Canyon counties) supports roughly **1.1–1.2 million residents** across approximately **430,000–450,000 housing units**, and Treasure Valley summers now routinely push **95–105°F** while winters deliver sustained **15–30°F** stretches — meaning nearly every household runs both cooling and heating systems hard. Typical residential HVAC economics in Boise: a **diagnostic/service call runs $89–$150**, a **standard AC tune-up $150–$275**, a **full system replacement $6,500–$14,000** (with heat pump conversions trending toward the upper half due to 2026 electrification incentives), and **commercial service contracts $2,400–$12,000/year per site**. A lean startup can realistically reach **$35,000–$60,000/month in revenue by month 12–18** with 2–3 trucks, assuming a **35–45% gross margin** on service work and **20–30% on installs**.

Entry costs are material but manageable: **Idaho HVAC contractor licensing, bonding, and insurance** ($2,500–$6,000), **one equipped service van** ($45,000–$75,000 new; $25,000–$40,000 used), **tools and recovery equipment** ($8,000–$15,000), and **EPA Section 608 certification** for refrigerant handling (individual certification, $300–$800 with prep). The competitive reality: Boise already hosts **60+ licensed HVAC contractors**, including regional heavyweights (Idaho Heating & Air, Western Heating & Air, A-1 Heating & Air) and a long tail of 2–10 truck shops. Winning means out-executing on **local search visibility, response speed, and review velocity** — not outspending incumbents.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Compliance & Foundation (Weeks 1–6)
1. **Register the LLC** with Idaho Secretary of State ($100).
2. **Obtain Idaho HVAC contractor license** through the Idaho Division of Building Safety; secure **general liability ($1M/$2M)** and **workers' comp**.
3. **Get EPA Section 608 certification** for all techs handling refrigerant.
4. **Secure a physical address** (home office acceptable initially; a small shop in Garden City or Meridian runs **$1,200–$2,500/mo**).
5. **Open business banking + merchant processing** (aim for <2.6% + $0.10 on card-present).

### Phase 2 — Digital Assets (Weeks 4–10, overlapping)
6. **Build the website** with service-area pages for Boise, Meridian, Nampa, Eagle, Kuna, Star, Caldwell. Include **LocalBusiness + HVACBusiness schema**, click-to-call, and online booking.
7. **Claim and optimize Google Business Profile** — categories, service areas, hours, photos, Q&A. This is your #1 lead source.
8. **Launch Google Local Services Ads** (Google Guaranteed badge) — budget **$1,500–$3,000/mo** through heating season.
9. **Stand up the CRM** (Jobber or Housecall Pro for 2–3 truck shops; ServiceTitan only at scale).
10. **Deploy review-generation automation** — text-after-service with direct Google review link.

### Phase 3 — Demand Generation (Weeks 8–12)
11. **Turn on Google Ads** for high-intent terms ("furnace repair Boise," "AC not cooling Meridian") — start at **$1,000–$2,000/mo**, tighten to top 10 keywords.
12. **Publish programmatic service+city pages** (e.g., "Heat Pump Installation in Eagle, ID") — 20–40 pages at launch, expand quarterly.
13. **Truck wraps + yard signs** in target subdivisions.
14. **Partner with 3–5 local realtors and property managers** for recurring maintenance contracts.

### Phase 4 — Scale & Retention (Months 4–12)
15. **Push annual maintenance plans** ($180–$400/home) — the recurring revenue that stabilizes cash flow.
16. **Track cost-per-booked-job weekly**; kill any channel above **$150/job** for service calls.
17. **Add a second truck** once service revenue clears **$25,000/month** consistently.
18. **Build commercial pipeline** (property managers, small retail) for winter contract work.
