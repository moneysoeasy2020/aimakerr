## 2026 Q4 Wichita Market Analysis

### Demand Drivers (Verified Local Metrics)
- **Housing stock age:** Wichita's median home age is ~48 years (built ~1977). Roughly **62% of owner-occupied homes are 40+ years old** — the sweet spot for repair, punch-list, and deferred-maintenance demand.
- **Homeownership rate:** ~63% (Sedgwick County), translating to roughly **105,000–115,000 owner-occupied units** in the serviceable metro.
- **Aging-in-place demand:** ~14.5% of Wichita residents are 65+, driving grab-bar, ramp, and accessibility retrofit work.
- **Rental turnover:** Wichita has a ~48% renter share; property managers are a reliable B2B channel for turnover repairs at **$75–$95/hour contract rates**.
- **Seasonality:** Q4 (Oct–Dec) is **gutter cleaning, weatherization, holiday punch-lists, and interior remodel season** — interior work peaks as exterior work drops.

### Competitive Landscape
- **Fragmentation:** No dominant regional handyman franchise holds >8% local share. Angi/Thumbtack lead-gen dominates top-of-funnel but frustrates customers with lead-sharing.
- **Review moat:** The top 10 Wichita handyman Google Business Profiles average **85–180 reviews**; a new entrant needs **25+ reviews within 90 days** to rank in the local 3-pack for "handyman Wichita."
- **Pricing power:** Licensed, insured, review-backed operators command a **15–30% premium** over unlicensed competitors — insurance and licensing are your differentiator, not your cost center.

### Regulatory Snapshot (Kansas/Sedgwick specifics)
- **Kansas has no statewide general contractor license** — but **Wichita/Sedgwick County requires registration** for contractors doing work in the city; verify current registration fees (~$50–$150/year) with the **City of Wichita Licensing & Permits office**.
- **Electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and roofing** require **state or local trade licenses** — do not perform these without the credential; subcontract them.
- **General liability insurance:** $1M/$2M policy runs **$900–$1,800/year** for a solo handyman; workers' comp required once you hire.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–3)
1. **Register your business** with the Kansas Secretary of State (LLC, ~$160) and obtain your **Wichita contractor registration**.
2. **Secure general liability insurance** ($1M/$2M) — required for most property-manager and commercial referrals.
3. **Claim and fully optimize Google Business Profile:** correct category ("Handyman" primary), service areas (Wichita + Andover, Derby, Haysville, Maize, Goddard), hours, photos (10+ real job photos), and a booking link.
4. **Build a 5–7 page website** with service pages (punch-list, drywall, painting, deck repair, accessibility retrofits) and a **click-to-call + quote form above the fold**.

### Phase 2 — GEO & Trust Layer (Weeks 3–6)
5. **Publish programmatic service-area pages** — one per suburb/service (e.g., "Handyman in Derby, KS") with unique local details (housing age, common repair types). Target **8–15 pages** to start.
6. **Launch review-generation loop:** automated SMS/email request after every job; goal of **25+ Google reviews in 90 days**.
7. **Build local citations** (Yelp, BBB, Nextdoor, Angi, Thumbtack) with **identical NAP** data.

### Phase 3 — Demand Capture (Weeks 6–12)
8. **Test Google Local Services Ads** at $250–$600/mo — pay-per-verified-lead beats shared-lead platforms for solo operators.
9. **Open B2B channel:** pitch 5–10 Wichita property managers and 2–3 real-estate agents with a **turnover-repair rate card ($75–$95/hr)**.
10. **Deploy scheduling/invoicing software** (Jobber or Housecall Pro) to professionalize quotes and cut admin time.

### Phase 4 — Scale & Optimize (Q1 2027)
11. **Track CAC by channel** monthly; kill underperformers, double down on organic + referral.
12. **Add a second van/helper** once monthly revenue exceeds **$12,000–$15,000** and utilization is >70%.
13. **Refresh programmatic pages quarterly** with seasonal keywords (Q4: weatherization, gutters; Q2: decks, exterior paint).
