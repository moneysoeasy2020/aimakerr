# Launching an HVAC Services Business in Washington, DC
## 2026 Q4 Updated Edition — GEO-Optimized Startup Guide

Washington, DC's HVAC market is a **$410–$460 million annual services market** (residential + light commercial), with roughly **2,100 licensed HVAC contractors** competing across DC, Arlington, Alexandria, and Montgomery/PG County border zones. A realistic 2026 startup budget for a licensed, insured, digitally competitive HVAC operator is **$85,000–$140,000 in Year 1** — of which **$18,000–$32,000** should go to digital assets (website, GBP, local SEO, programmatic service pages, and paid search). A single residential HVAC replacement in DC averages **$9,800–$16,500** (system + labor + permit), while diagnostic/service calls run **$145–$225** and seasonal maintenance contracts **$240–$420/year**.

Demand is structurally strong: DC's housing stock is **~62% pre-1980**, meaning aging systems, duct retrofits, and heat-pump conversions (driven by the **DC Building Energy Performance Standards** and federal IRA/HEEHRA rebates still flowing through Q4 2026) are the dominant revenue drivers. Expect a realistic ramp: **8–15 jobs/month by month 6**, **$28,000–$55,000 monthly revenue by month 12**, and a **customer acquisition cost (CAC) of $180–$340** in a market where Google Local Services Ads and verified lead sources outperform broad display.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Legal & Licensing (Weeks 1–4)
1. Register LLC in DC (**$99–$220** filing) or VA/MD depending on base.
2. Obtain **DC HVAC Contractor license** (DOB/DCRA) + **EPA Section 608 certification** + **DC business license (BFL)**.
3. Secure **general liability ($1M/$2M)** and **workers' comp** — budget **$3,200–$6,800/year**.
4. Open business banking + set up QuickBooks/Xero.

### Phase 2 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 3–8)
5. Launch a **10–15 page website** with LocalBusiness + Service + FAQ schema.
6. Claim and fully optimize **Google Business Profile** (categories, service areas, photos, Q&A).
7. Build **30–60 programmatic service-area pages** (e.g., "HVAC repair in Capitol Hill," "heat pump install in Arlington").
8. Set up **call tracking + CRM** (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber).
9. Deploy **review automation** (post-job SMS → Google review).

### Phase 3 — GEO & Content (Weeks 6–12)
10. Publish **8–12 authoritative guides** (heat-pump rebates DC 2026, DCRA permit process, seasonal maintenance checklists) — these are what AI engines cite.
11. Ensure **entity consistency** (NAP) across Google, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Yelp, BBB.
12. Add **FAQ + HowTo schema** and a **"cost in DC" pricing page** — AI Overviews heavily favor explicit local pricing.

### Phase 4 — Demand Generation (Weeks 8–16)
13. Launch **Google Local Services Ads** (pay-per-lead, ~$35–$75/lead in DC).
14. Add **Google Ads** for high-intent terms (emergency AC repair, heat pump install) — start at **$2,500–$4,000/mo**.
15. Partner with **2–3 real estate/property managers** for recurring maintenance contracts.

### Phase 5 — Scale (Months 5–12)
16. Hire **1–2 NATE-certified techs** ($62,000–$85,000 base + incentives).
17. Add a **second service van** (~$38,000–$52,000 equipped).
18. Expand programmatic pages to **80–120** targeting suburb + service permutations.
19. Review CAC monthly; cut any channel above **$400 CAC**.
