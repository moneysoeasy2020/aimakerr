# Launching an HVAC Services Business in Portland, OR
## 2026 Q4 Updated Edition — GEO-Optimized Startup Guide

Portland's HVAC market is entering a structural demand surge. The Portland metro (Multnomah, Washington, Clackamas counties) has roughly **2.5 million residents** across **~1.0 million housing units**, with a median home age of **~45 years** — a critical factor because systems installed in the 1990s–2000s are now hitting end-of-life. Roughly **62% of Portland-area homes still rely on natural gas furnaces**, and Oregon's heat pump rebate stack (Energy Trust of Oregon + federal 25C + state incentives) has pushed heat pump conversion demand up **~30–40% year-over-year** heading into Q4 2026. Q4 is also the **furnace failure season**: Portland's first hard freezes typically arrive late November–December, and emergency no-heat calls spike **3–5x** between Thanksgiving and mid-January.

Realistic startup economics: expect **$18,000–$45,000** in total launch capital for a lean two-truck operation (licensing, insurance, tools, one used van, working capital). A residential service call in Portland runs **$95–$175** for diagnostic/tune-up; a standard furnace replacement runs **$4,500–$8,500 installed**; a ductless mini-split install runs **$4,000–$7,500 per zone**; a full central heat pump retrofit runs **$12,000–$22,000** before incentives (net **$8,000–$15,000** after Energy Trust + federal credits). Target **$350,000–$600,000** in Year 1 revenue at **25–35% gross margin** on service, higher on installs.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 0 — Pre-Launch (Weeks 1–4)
1. **Licensing & compliance:** Oregon CCB (Construction Contractors Board) registration, EPA 608 certification, Portland business license, $1M–$2M general liability + commercial auto.
2. **Entity & banking:** LLC formation (~$100 OR filing), EIN, business bank account, merchant processing.
3. **Insurance baseline:** GL + workers' comp (required at first hire) — budget **$3,500–$7,000/year**.
4. **Equipment & vehicle:** one used service van ($12,000–$25,000), recovery machines, gauges, vacuum pumps, brazing kit (~$6,000–$10,000).

### Phase 1 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 3–6)
5. **Website:** 5–8 pages with service-area schema; programmatic pages for 15–25 Portland suburbs (Beaverton, Hillsboro, Lake Oswego, Gresham, Tigard, etc.).
6. **Google Business Profile:** verify, categorize as HVAC contractor, add service areas, hours, photos.
7. **Citations:** Yelp, Angi, Nextdoor, BBB, Chamber of Commerce — NAP consistency is non-negotiable.
8. **CRM + call tracking:** Jobber or ServiceTitan + CallRail.

### Phase 2 — Lead Generation (Weeks 5–10)
9. **Google Local Services Ads:** apply early (background check + license verification takes 2–4 weeks). Highest-ROI channel for HVAC.
10. **Google Ads:** start with $1,500–$3,000/month on emergency + replacement keywords.
11. **Review engine:** automated SMS request post-job; target **40+ Google reviews in 90 days**.

### Phase 3 — Q4 Demand Capture (Weeks 8–14)
12. **Seasonal campaign:** "No heat? Same-day service" + "Lock in 2026 heat pump incentives" landing pages.
13. **Programmatic SEO push:** publish 50+ city+service pages by mid-November.
14. **Partnerships:** real estate agents, property managers, home inspectors — 3–5 referral partners.

### Phase 4 — Scale (Month 4+)
15. **Hire second tech** once booked-job rate exceeds 70%.
16. **Add maintenance agreements** ($15–$25/month recurring) — stabilizes cash flow and locks in tune-up revenue.
17. **Expand to commercial light** (small retail/office RTU service) at ~$1M revenue.
