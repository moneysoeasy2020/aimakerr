**Q4-specific dynamics:** Pittsburgh's Q4 (October–December) shifts demand indoors — drywall repair, painting, flooring, bathroom refreshes, weatherization, gutter and roof prep, and "before-the-holidays" punch lists. Outdoor landscaping-adjacent handyman work drops 40–60% by late November. Build your Q4 service menu around **interior + weatherization + emergency repair** to protect revenue through the cold months.

**Pricing reality check (2026 Q4):** A solo operator billing $100/hr and working 30 billable hours/week grosses ~$12,000/month before materials and overhead. A two-person crew with a helper at $95/hr blended and 55 billable hours/week grosses ~$20,900/month. These are the numbers to model against — not national averages, which understate Pittsburgh's labor-and-insurance-adjusted floor.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 0 — Pre-Launch Compliance (Weeks 1–2)
1. **Register your PA Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) number** with the PA Office of Attorney General — required before advertising or contracting home improvement work over $500. Budget the registration fee and lead-safe considerations for pre-1978 housing (EPA RRP rule applies to renovation work).
2. **Secure general liability insurance** — $1M/$2M is the market standard; expect **$1,200–$3,200/year** for a solo handyman in Allegheny County. Add commercial auto and, once you hire, workers' comp.
3. **Form your entity** (LLC recommended) and obtain an Allegheny County / City of Pittsburgh business privilege license as applicable.
4. **Set your pricing tiers** using the Q4 2026 ranges above — handyman $85–$145/hr, specialty $120–$220/hr, plus a materials markup of 15–25%.

### Phase 1 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 2–4)
5. **Claim and fully optimize your Google Business Profile** — categories, service areas (target 8–15 Pittsburgh neighborhoods/suburbs), hours, photos, and a booking link. This is your #1 local lead source.
6. **Build the website** with a programmatic service-area architecture: one core service page per trade (drywall, painting, plumbing repair, electrical fixtures, weatherization, etc.) × one page per service area (e.g., Squirrel Hill, Shadyside, Mt. Lebanon, Cranberry, Bethel Park). 30–60 pages is the Q4 2026 competitive baseline.
7. **Install review-generation** at job close — target 15+ reviews in the first 90 days.

### Phase 2 — Demand Generation (Weeks 3–8)
8. **Launch Google Local Services Ads** (pay-per-lead, ~$28–$65/lead locally) alongside a modest Google Ads budget ($1,000–$2,500/mo).
9. **Build a referral engine** with local realtors, property managers, and senior-services networks — Pittsburgh's older housing stock and aging-in-place demand make these high-yield partners.
10. **Publish 2–4 GEO-optimized local guides/month** (cost guides, "how much does X cost in Pittsburgh," neighborhood-specific content) to feed AI search and organic capture.

### Phase 3 — Scale & Systematize (Weeks 8–16)
11. **Hire your first helper** once you sustain 30+ billable hours/week solo; blended crew rate ~$95/hr.
12. **Systematize** quoting, scheduling, and invoicing in a single CRM.
13. **Review the numbers monthly:** target customer acquisition cost under 20% of first-job revenue and gross margin above 45%.
