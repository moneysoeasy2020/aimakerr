## Executive Snapshot: Louisville HVAC Market Numbers (2026 Q4)

The Louisville–Jefferson County metro (population ~1.29M as of 2026) supports roughly **340–380 licensed HVAC contractors**, with the Louisville Metro Department of Codes & Regulations reporting **~$48M–$62M in annual residential HVAC replacement permits** and an average of **11,400–13,200 HVAC permit filings per year**. Average residential system replacement runs **$6,800–$12,400** (3-ton split system, full install), service calls average **$185–$340**, and seasonal maintenance plans typically price at **$149–$289/year**. Commercial rooftop unit (RTU) service contracts in the metro average **$2,400–$9,800/year per site**.

Startup capital to launch a compliant, marketable Louisville HVAC company in Q4 2026 realistically ranges from **$42,000 (lean, owner-operator, used van)** to **$118,000 (two crews, branded fleet, digital-first)**. Kentucky requires a **Master HVAC license** through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction (DHBC), a **$5,000–$10,000 surety bond**, general liability insurance at **$1M/$2M minimum**, and Louisville Metro business licensing — total licensing and compliance cost lands at **$3,200–$5,800** before first revenue.

## 2026 Q4 Louisville Market Analysis

### Demand Drivers Specific to Louisville
- **Climate profile:** Louisville sits in ASHRAE Climate Zone 4A — hot-humid summers (design temp ~92°F) and cold winters (~12°F design). This dual-season load means **both heating and cooling equipment cycles heavily**, driving 2x annual service touchpoints per household.
- **Housing stock:** ~**62% of metro housing units are pre-2000 construction**, with high concentrations of 1980s–1990s builds in Jeffersontown, Fern Creek, and Okolona now hitting the **25–35 year replacement window**.
- **Heat pump transition:** Federal 25C tax credits (extended through 2032 under current statute) plus LG&E/KU rebates of **$300–$1,200 per qualifying install** are accelerating heat pump adoption — Louisville heat pump installs grew **~34% YoY in 2025–2026**.
- **Competitive density:** Per capita contractor density is **lower than Nashville or Indianapolis**, leaving underserved pockets in **Shively, Pleasure Ridge Park, and Newburg** where average response times exceed 4 hours.

### Seasonal Revenue Curve (Q4 2026 Entry)
Launching in Q4 means entering at the **furnace tune-up and emergency heat season**, which historically delivers **38–44% of annual residential service revenue between November and February** in Louisville. This is the optimal quarter to build a customer list before the spring AC rush.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1 — Compliance & Entity (Weeks 1–4)
1. Register LLC with Kentucky Secretary of State (**$40 filing fee**).
2. Obtain **Master HVAC license** via DHBC (exam + experience verification; allow 4–8 weeks).
3. Secure **$5,000–$10,000 surety bond** and **$1M/$2M GL insurance** (~$2,400–$4,100/yr for a 2-van operation).
4. File Louisville Metro business license and Jefferson County occupational tax registration.
5. Set up EPA Section 608 certification for refrigerant handling (required for all techs).

### Phase 2 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 3–8)
1. Launch website with **service-area pages for 15 priority zip codes** (40205, 40218, 40220, 40229, 40258, 40272, 40291, 40299, 40059, 47130, etc.).
2. Optimize Google Business Profile with **Louisville-specific service categories** and 10+ geo-tagged job photos.
3. Build 25 core citations (Yelp, Angi, BBB Louisville, Nextdoor, Chamber of Commerce).
4. Deploy call tracking with **dynamic number insertion** per channel.

### Phase 3 — Demand Generation (Weeks 6–12)
1. Activate **Google Local Services Ads** ($900–$1,800/mo starting budget; HVAC LSA cost-per-lead in Louisville runs **$28–$65**).
2. Launch review-generation SMS flow — target **25 Google reviews by day 90** (top-quartile for new Louisville HVAC entrants).
3. Programmatic content: publish **15–25 service+city pages** (e.g., "Furnace Repair in Jeffersontown, KY") at $45–$110/page via verified freelancer.
4. Direct mail to pre-2000 housing subdivisions in Fern Creek and PRP (**$0.62–$0.89/piece** at 2,500-piece volume).

### Phase 4 — Retention & Scale (Month 4+)
1. Launch **$189/year maintenance plan** with 2 seasonal visits — target 15% attach rate on service calls.
2. Add commercial RTU contracts in Bluegrass Industrial Park and River Ridge corridor (**$2,400–$9,800/site/year**).
3. Reinvest 8–12% of revenue into digital; renegotiate freelancer scope quarterly against a **cost-per-booked-job KPI** (target: under $95 residential, under $340 commercial).
