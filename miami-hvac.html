# Launching an HVAC Services Business in Miami, FL
## 2026 Q4 Updated Edition — GEO-Optimized Startup Guide

---

Miami-Dade County's HVAC services market generated approximately **$1.42 billion in 2025 revenue** across roughly **1,850 licensed contractors**, with the residential segment (retrofit + repair) accounting for **~62%** of that spend. The average Miami homeowner spends **$380–$650 per year** on HVAC maintenance and repair, while full system replacements in the 2026 market run **$7,200–$14,500** for a standard 3-ton split system (SEER2 15.2 minimum per DOE 2023 rules) and **$18,000–$32,000** for a variable-speed multi-zone install in a 2,000–2,800 sq ft Miami home. Commercial rooftop unit (RTU) replacements in Miami-Dade average **$11,500–$26,000 per unit** depending on tonnage and crane access.

Entry costs to launch a compliant Miami HVAC business in Q4 2026 are realistic and well-documented: **Florida DBPR Certified Air Conditioning Contractor license (CAC)** application and exam fees total **~$1,150–$1,800**, general liability insurance ($1M/$2M) runs **$2,800–$4,600/year**, a used service van + tools package is **$28,000–$42,000**, EPA Section 608 Universal certification is **$350–$600**, and Miami-Dade Local Business Tax Receipt + City of Miami (or unincorporated) BTR adds **$180–$420**. Total realistic startup capital: **$48,000–$78,000** for a lean owner-operator, or **$120,000–$210,000** for a 2-technician shop with a dispatcher.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan (Q4 2026 Launch)

### Phase 1 — Licensing & Compliance (Weeks 1–6)
1. Register LLC with **Florida Division of Corporations** ($125)
2. Apply for **DBPR CAC license** (or work under a qualifying agent if you're new) — exam + fingerprint + background: **~$1,150–$1,800**
3. Obtain **EPA 608 Universal** certification ($350–$600)
4. Secure **general liability ($1M/$2M)** + commercial auto + workers' comp (if hiring): **$3,400–$6,200/year**
5. File **Miami-Dade Local BTR** + city BTR: **$180–$420**
6. Register with **Florida Sunbiz** for sales tax on parts (if applicable)

### Phase 2 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 3–8)
7. Build a **GEO-optimized website** with HVAC schema, service-area pages, and FAQ blocks citing Miami-specific data
8. Claim and optimize **Google Business Profile** (service-area business, 15+ photos, review request workflow)
9. Build **60–120 programmatic neighborhood pages** (Kendall, Doral, Hialeah, Brickell, Little Havana, Homestead, Aventura, etc.)
10. Set up **LSA + Google Ads** with a $3,000–$6,000/month starting budget

### Phase 3 — Operations & Launch (Weeks 6–10)
11. Procure **used service van + tools** ($28,000–$42,000) or lease ($620–$980/mo)
12. Set up **field service software** (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber): $150–$450/mo
13. Establish **parts supplier accounts** (Carrier, Trane, Rheem distributors; Ferguson HVAC; Johnstone Supply)
14. Hire **1–2 EPA-certified techs** (Miami market rate: $24–$34/hr + benefits)
15. Launch with **$3,500–$6,500/month paid acquisition** for first 90 days

### Phase 4 — Scale (Months 4–12)
16. Add **commercial PM contracts** (target 8–15 accounts at $1,400–$3,200/yr each)
17. Build **review velocity**: target 25–40 Google reviews in first 6 months
18. Expand programmatic SEO to **Broward + Palm Beach** (adjacent high-value markets)
19. Target **$480,000–$780,000 Year-1 revenue** for a 2-tech shop (industry benchmark for Miami owner-operators)
