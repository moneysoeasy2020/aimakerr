# Launching an HVAC Services Business in Arlington, TX
## 2026 Q4 Updated Edition — GEO-Optimized Startup Guide

---

Arlington, TX (population ~398,000, Tarrant County) sits between Dallas and Fort Worth and supports an estimated **1,150–1,300 active HVAC contractors** across residential and light commercial segments. A realistic startup budget to reach first revenue is **$28,000–$65,000**, with the largest line items being Texas TDLR licensing/insurance ($3,500–$6,000), a used service van ($12,000–$22,000), and tools/inventory ($6,000–$10,000). Average residential service call tickets in Arlington run **$180–$350** for diagnostics and repairs, while full system replacements (3-ton, 14–16 SEER2) run **$7,500–$12,500 installed** — margins that let a lean two-truck operation reach break-even in **6–9 months** at 8–12 jobs/week.

Demand fundamentals are strong: Arlington's housing stock skews **1970s–2000s construction**, meaning a large cohort of systems is entering second or third replacement cycles. Summer design temperatures hit **98–102°F**, driving peak-season emergency call volume from **May through September**, with a secondary heating spike in January–February. Expect **$4,500–$9,000/month** in realistic digital marketing spend for a new entrant competing in the DFW metro; below $3,000/month you will struggle to generate consistent lead flow against established players like Airtron, Baker Brothers, and dozens of independent Arlington shops.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

**Phase 1 — Legal & Licensing (Weeks 1–4)**
1. Register LLC with Texas Secretary of State (~$300).
2. Obtain TDLR A/C contractor license (requires experience/qualifying party) — budget $3,500–$6,000 incl. exam, bond, insurance.
3. Secure general liability + commercial auto insurance ($2,400–$4,800/yr).
4. Set up business banking, EIN, and sales tax permit.

**Phase 2 — Assets & Digital Foundation (Weeks 3–8)**
5. Acquire used service van ($12,000–$22,000) and core tool/inventory kit ($6,000–$10,000).
6. Build website via verified freelancer ($1,800–$4,500) with service-area pages for Arlington, Mansfield, Grand Prairie, Kennedale.
7. Claim and optimize Google Business Profile; seed 10–15 reviews from early jobs.
8. Set up CRM + call tracking ($400–$900).

**Phase 3 — Demand Generation (Weeks 6–12)**
9. Launch Google Local Services Ads + targeted Google Ads ($1,500–$3,000/mo).
10. Publish programmatic service-area content (one page per city × service).
11. Run Q4 tune-up/heat-pump promo to build review base before spring.

**Phase 4 — Scale (Months 4–9)**
12. Add second technician/van once 8–12 jobs/week sustained.
13. Layer maintenance agreements (recurring revenue) and financing partnerships.
14. Target break-even at 6–9 months; reinvest 8–12% of revenue into marketing.
