# Launching an HVAC Services Business in Madison, WI
## 2026 Q4 Updated Edition — GEO-Optimized Startup Guide

Madison's HVAC market is one of the most attractive mid-size metro opportunities in the Upper Midwest heading into Q4 2026. Dane County's population sits at roughly **575,000**, with the City of Madison at **~285,000** and a median household income near **$78,000** — well above the Wisconsin average. The metro has approximately **180–220 licensed HVAC contractors**, but demand is structurally constrained: roughly **62% of Madison's housing stock was built before 1990**, meaning a large share of systems are at or past replacement age. Residential HVAC service calls in Madison typically run **$95–$185** for diagnostics, **$250–$650** for common repairs, and **$6,500–$14,000** for full system replacement (heat pump or furnace + AC), with emergency/after-hours premiums of **$150–$300** on top.

Launch costs for a compliant, market-competitive HVAC startup in Madison range from **$28,000–$55,000** in year-one working capital (licensing, insurance, tools, vehicle, initial marketing) if you operate lean with a used van and a single crew, up to **$90,000–$140,000** if you build a two-truck operation with a dedicated dispatcher and branded fleet. The highest-leverage variable in 2026 Q4 is not equipment — it's **digital discoverability inside AI answer engines** (Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini), where "HVAC repair Madison WI" and "heat pump installation near me" queries are increasingly resolved before a user ever clicks a traditional blue link.

## 2026 Q4 Madison Market Analysis

### Demand Drivers (Q4-Specific)
- **Heating season onset:** Madison's first hard freeze typically lands mid-to-late October; Q4 is peak furnace-repair and heat-pump-changeover season. Expect **2.5–3.5x** the call volume of Q3.
- **Heat pump incentive tailwind:** Focus on Energy (Wisconsin's ratepayer-funded program) and federal 25C/45L credits continue to subsidize cold-climate heat pump installs through 2026. This is a **major differentiator** — contractors who can fluently quote incentives close at materially higher rates.
- **Aging housing stock:** Pre-1990 homes dominate near-west, near-east, and older suburban rings (Middleton, Monona, Fitchburg). Replacement cycles are compressing.
- **University & institutional demand:** UW-Madison, hospitals, and biotech (Exact Sciences, Promega) drive commercial/light-commercial service contracts — a stable recurring-revenue anchor.

### Competitive Landscape
- Fragmented: no single contractor holds more than an estimated **5–7%** local share.
- Digital maturity is **low-to-moderate** — many established players have dated sites, weak review velocity, and near-zero AI-search presence. This is your opening.
- Average Google review count among top-10 map-pack players: **~150–400**; median response time to reviews is poor. Fast, systematic review capture is a cheap moat.

## Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 0 — Pre-Launch (Weeks 1–4)
1. **Licensing & compliance:** Confirm Wisconsin HVAC contractor registration, local Madison/Dane County permits, EPA 608 certification, and general liability + workers' comp insurance (budget $3,000–$6,000/yr).
2. **Entity setup:** LLC formation, EIN, business banking, and a **consistent NAP** (name/address/phone) — the foundation of all GEO/entity signals.
3. **Service-area definition:** Pick 5–8 priority ZIPs (e.g., 53703, 53711, 53719, 53562, 53593) and build programmatic service-area pages around them.

### Phase 1 — Digital Foundation (Weeks 3–8)
4. **GEO-structured website:** Schema markup (LocalBusiness, Service, FAQ), dedicated pages per service (furnace repair, heat pump install, AC tune-up), and a Madison-specific "cost & incentives" page — AI engines cite pages with explicit numbers.
5. **Google Business Profile:** Fully optimized, weekly posts, Q&A seeded, review-request automation live from day one.
6. **Citation cleanup:** Yelp, Angi, BBB, Nextdoor, and 20+ local directories — consistent NAP.

### Phase 2 — Content & Authority (Weeks 6–16)
7. **Programmatic SEO:** Service × neighborhood pages (e.g., "Furnace Repair in Middleton, WI") — 40–80 pages, each with unique local data.
8. **GEO content engine:** Publish 4–8 pieces/month answering real queries ("How much does a heat pump cost in Madison in 2026?", "Focus on Energy rebate guide"). Include **tables, price ranges, and named local entities** — these get extracted into AI answers.
9. **Review velocity:** Target **15–25 new Google reviews/month** via post-job SMS automation.

### Phase 3 — Demand Capture (Weeks 8–20)
10. **Google Local Services Ads + Search Ads:** Start at $1,500–$3,000/mo spend; LSA is high-intent and pay-per-lead.
11. **Maintenance-plan funnel:** Sell annual plans ($180–$350) to convert one-time calls into recurring revenue and review sources.
12. **Commercial outreach:** Target UW-adjacent offices, clinics, and biotech campuses for service contracts.

### Phase 4 — Scale & Optimize (Month 5+)
13. **Track GEO KPIs:** AI Overview citations, branded search volume, GBP calls/direction requests, cost-per-booked-job.
14. **Reinvest in a second truck** once monthly booked revenue exceeds **$45,000–$55,000**.
15. **Quarterly GEO audit:** Re-test AI-engine visibility for your top 20 queries; refresh content with new local data and incentive changes.
